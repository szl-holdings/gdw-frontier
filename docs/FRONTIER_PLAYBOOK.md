# New Frontier operating playbook

Status: **MODELED**

This playbook is an original synthesis for GDW operators. It borrows no brand
identity, garment design, source code, or distinctive wording. It translates
widely used leadership, design, circularity, systems, reliability, and risk
management patterns into a governed operating loop.

## The Frontier Loop

### 1. Sense

Start with observed conditions, not the desired story. Combine quantitative
signals with operator and affected-party perspectives. Keep disagreements
visible long enough to learn from them.

**GDW evidence:** bounded metrics, structured logs, trace correlation, current
truth labels, and an explicit statement of unavailable evidence.

### 2. Frame

Define the decision, intended users, affected parties, constraints, failure
surface, and stop conditions. Separate the problem from the first proposed
solution.

**GDW evidence:** a scoped change statement, risk boundary, ownership, and
rollback trigger.

### 3. Diverge

Develop multiple plausible responses and scenarios. Reward cognitive
flexibility and informed dissent. A single unchallenged plan is a risk signal,
not a mark of alignment.

**GDW evidence:** alternatives considered, assumptions, and the reason for
selecting one path.

### 4. Prototype

Run the smallest reversible experiment that can falsify an important
assumption. Keep the experiment isolated from production authority.

**GDW evidence:** deterministic fixture, local or sandbox execution, bounded
resource budget, and cleanup path.

### 5. Prove

Measure the result against predeclared acceptance and failure criteria. Keep
`MODELED`, `MEASURED`, `VERIFIED`, and `UNAVAILABLE` distinct. A running
service, HTTP 200, or attractive dashboard is not production proof.

**GDW evidence:** test output, signal-path receipt, artifact hashes, exact
source commit, and stated verification scope.

### 6. Circulate

Treat operational artifacts the way circular design treats products: make
them durable, repairable, reusable, and safe to disassemble. Prefer modular
contracts, replaceable components, documented ownership, and governed
retirement over one-use integrations.

**GDW evidence:** versioned schemas, reusable runbooks, pinned dependencies,
artifact manifests, migration paths, and decommissioning instructions.

### 7. Govern

Record the decision, residual risk, accountable owner, review date, and
rollback path. Feed production observations into the next Sense phase.
Governance is continuous and cross-cutting, not a ceremony at the end.

**GDW evidence:** decision receipt, human authority boundary, alert ownership,
review cadence, and a fail-closed response when required evidence is absent.

## Leadership contract

- Leaders create direction, alignment, and commitment without suppressing
  dissent.
- Adaptability means changing the plan when evidence changes, while preserving
  truth and safety constraints.
- Psychological safety is operational: people must be able to report bad
  signals, uncertainty, and mistakes without the system hiding them.
- Optimism must stay grounded in measured reality.
- Human accountability cannot be delegated to an automated recommendation.

## Design and fashion contract

- Use fashion as a lesson in composition, identity, lifecycle, and repair, not
  as a library of looks to copy.
- Build a recognizable GDW voice from original tokens, language, and
  interactions.
- Design components to be maintained, reused, remade, or retired.
- Record the origin, license, and allowed use of every third-party asset.
- Do not imitate a living designer's signature style or reproduce protected
  brand elements.

## Release questions

Every frontier release answers these questions:

1. What changed in the observed environment?
2. Who benefits, who could be harmed, and who can stop the release?
3. Which alternatives were considered?
4. What is the smallest reversible experiment?
5. What was personally executed and observed?
6. Which artifacts can be reused, repaired, or retired?
7. What remains `MODELED`, `UNVERIFIED`, or `UNAVAILABLE`?
8. Who owns residual risk and the rollback?
